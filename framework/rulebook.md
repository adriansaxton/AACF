# Assessor rulebook

These rules make results repeatable: two assessors with the same evidence should reach the same level.

## 1. Scope first

1. Record the boundary of the assessment: which parts of the organisation, and which uses of AI.
2. Record which overlays apply (PUB, PRV, HIR, GEN, AGT, BLD), with a one-line reason for each decision.
3. A criterion tied to an overlay that does not apply is left out. It is neither met nor failed.
   A criterion tagged with more than one overlay applies when **any** of them applies.
4. A whole control is N/A only when every one of its criteria is tied to overlays that do not apply, or when the
   assessor records a specific reason that is approved by the assessment sponsor.

## 2. Statuses before a level

Every control ends the assessment with exactly one status:

| Status | Use when | Counts in scores? |
|---|---|---|
| **Scored (0 to 5)** | Enough evidence was seen to place the control at a level | Yes |
| **Insufficient evidence** | The control was looked at, but the evidence could not support a level | No. It is listed separately, and is not treated as 0 |
| **Not assessed** | The control was not looked at in this assessment | No. It is listed separately |
| **N/A** | Rule 1.4 applies, with the reason recorded | No |

Level 0 means **confirmed absence**: the evidence shows that nothing is in place. "We don't know" is *insufficient
evidence*, not 0.

## 3. Placing the level

1. **Level 3:** every applicable Level 3 criterion is met. One unmet applicable criterion means the control is not
   at Level 3.
2. **Levels 4 and 5:** Level 3 must be met first. Each higher level must then be fully met on its own descriptor.
   Levels are cumulative and are never skipped.
3. **Levels 1 and 2:** use the descriptors. As a rule, Level 2 is in place in part of the organisation, or in place but
   not enforced or not owned. Level 1 is individual effort with nothing documented.
4. **Never round up.** If the evidence sits between two levels, record the lower one and note what is missing.
5. **Record every criterion.** For each applicable Level 3 criterion, record met, not met or not evidenced, so a
   partial result shows exactly what is outstanding.

## 4. Evidence

1. Each scored control has at least one evidence reference. A level of 3 or more with no evidence reference is
   invalid.
2. **Recency:** evidence should be current, which normally means within the last 12 months or the last review cycle.
   Older evidence needs a note explaining why it still holds.
3. **Sampling:** where a criterion applies to "each use", sample enough uses to be confident: all of them if there
   are five or fewer, otherwise at least five, chosen to include higher-risk uses. Record what was sampled.
4. **Interviews alone** can support Level 1 or 2. Level 3 needs a record.
5. **Contradictory evidence:** go with the evidence that shows actual practice (registers, logs, completed records)
   over evidence that shows intent (policies, plans), and note the conflict.
6. **Inherited controls** (a parent body's or a supplier's) count where the organisation can show they apply to it and
   it can rely on them, for example through a written agreement or assurance report.
7. **Confidence:** record high, medium or low for each scored control. Low confidence is reported, and is not hidden
   in the level.

## 5. Targets

1. The default target is 3, the baseline.
2. A target **below 3** needs a recorded rationale, an approver and a review date. A criterion that is required by
   law or regulation cannot be lowered by a local target.
3. Results are always reported twice: **against the Level 3 baseline** and **against the agreed target**. Lowering
   a target never changes the baseline result.

## 6. Independence

1. The person who operates a control should not be the only person who scores it. Assurance and monitoring controls
   (the MON domain) should be scored or challenged by someone independent of their operation.
2. Record the assessor, and the reviewer or challenger, for each scored control in the higher-risk domains (GOV,
   RSK, SEC), and for any control scored at Level 4 or 5.

## 7. Reporting

Lead with the **distribution of levels** and the **share of controls at the Level 3 baseline**, then the share at
target, the counts of insufficient evidence, not assessed and N/A, and the critical gaps. The mean level may be shown
for trends, but only as a descriptive average: the 0 to 5 scale is ordered, and the steps between levels are not
equal.
