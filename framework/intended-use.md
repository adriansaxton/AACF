# Intended use, claims and terms

## What the framework is for

The AI Adoption Controls Framework sets out what an organisation needs in place to adopt AI well. It covers who owns
each control, how to tell how far along the organisation is, and how its controls line up with published standards.
It is for:

- leaders and AI governance leads who want a clear picture of where the organisation stands;
- assessors, internal audit and consultants who assess an organisation against it;
- teams who want a practical route to a defensible baseline.

## What is assessed

The unit of assessment is **the organisation**, or a clearly defined part of it, such as a division or a group of
services. An assessment is not a test of one AI system. Many criteria apply to "each use of AI", so an assessor
checks that the practice is in place and applied across the uses in scope, sampling where the number of uses is
large.

An assessment starts with **scoping**: what is in scope, and which overlays apply (see below). It then scores each
control from 0 to 5 using the rules in the assessor rulebook.

## What a result means, and what it does not

- A control **at Level 3** means the organisation meets every applicable Level 3 criterion for that control, on the
  evidence the assessor saw, at the date of the assessment.
- A result is a **framework assessment**. It is not certification against any standard and not a legal compliance
  opinion. It does not show that the organisation complies with ISO/IEC 42001, the EU AI Act or any other mapped
  source.
- **Cross-references** show where a control supports a source requirement, as the author interprets it. They help
  an organisation see where one piece of evidence may serve several purposes. They are not a substitute for reading
  the source.
- **"Coverage of mapped items"** describes the items selected from each source for mapping. It does not claim that
  every obligation in a source is covered.

## Overlays

Every criterion is either **core**, applying to every organisation, or **conditional** on an overlay. During scoping
the organisation records which overlays apply and why. A conditional criterion counts only when its overlay applies.

| Code | Overlay | Applies when the organisation |
|---|---|---|
| PUB | Public body | is a public sector body, or acts on behalf of one, with public duties such as transparency and equality |
| PRV | Provides AI to others | offers AI services, products or models to customers, partners or the public |
| HIR | Regulated or high-impact use | has uses of AI that are regulated, high-risk under law, or could significantly affect people's rights, safety or access to services |
| GEN | Generative AI | uses AI that generates text, images, audio, video or code |
| AGT | Agentic AI | uses AI that can act in other systems or act with some autonomy, including through tools |
| BLD | Builds or tunes its own models | trains, fine-tunes or otherwise develops models or AI components itself |

## Glossary

| Term | Meaning in this framework |
|---|---|
| **Use of AI** | A distinct application of AI to a purpose in the organisation, whether built, bought, embedded in another product or used as a general tool. The unit recorded in the AI inventory (GOV-06). |
| **In scope** | Within the boundary set at scoping. "Every use of AI" means every use within that boundary. |
| **Higher risk** | A use rated at the upper tiers of the organisation's own risk classification (RSK-01, RSK-02), or one covered by the HIR overlay. |
| **Provider** | An organisation that develops AI, or has it developed, and places it on the market or provides it to others under its own name. |
| **Deployer** | An organisation that uses AI under its own authority, whoever built it. |
| **AI from others** | AI the organisation obtains from suppliers, including AI features embedded in products it buys. |
| **Evidence** | A record that shows a practice exists and is applied: a document, a register entry, a log, a decision record, a sample of completed work, or a corroborated interview. |
| **Criterion** | One testable outcome within a control's Level 3. Each has an ID such as GOV-01.2. |
| **Baseline** | Level 3. The point at which a control is met. |
| **Target** | The level the organisation has agreed to reach for a control. Normally 3. A target below 3 needs a recorded rationale, an approver and a review date. |
| **At target** | The current level equals or exceeds the agreed target. This is different from reaching the baseline. |
