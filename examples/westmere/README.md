# Worked example: Westmere Standards Agency

Westmere Standards Agency is an invented UK arm's-length public body of about 2,000 staff that sets and checks technical standards for a regulated industry. No real body, client or employer material is used.

A year into AI adoption, it has Microsoft 365 Copilot for 600 staff and two pilots run by a data team of six: a correspondence triage classifier it tuned itself, due live in January 2027, and a guidance search assistant. The AI policy is a draft, there is no inventory and no transparency record. Information assurance and information governance are strong.

## What the example shows

- **Overlays.** Public body, generative AI and builds its own models apply; the other three do not, each with a reason. Criteria tied to an overlay that does not apply are left out.
- **Levels from criteria.** Each control below Level 3 lists the criteria it does not meet. Most sit at 1 or 2; Level 3 appears only where security and information governance meet every criterion, plus one Level 4 (SEC-04).
- **Statuses.** Level 0 means confirmed absence. OPM-10 and CUL-04 are *insufficient evidence*: not scored and not counted as 0. Nothing is N/A, as no control has all its criteria tied to overlays that do not apply.
- **Baseline and target.** Four targets of 2 carry a rationale, approver and review date. POR-08 is at target but below the baseline.
- **Confidence and independence.** Every control has a confidence rating and an assessor; governance, risk, security and monitoring controls also name a reviewer.
- **Actions.** Eight priority actions with named owners, dates and status, led by work due before triage goes live.

## Files

- `responses.yaml`: the example assessment (scores, evidence, statuses, actions).
- `report.md`: the written report produced from it.
